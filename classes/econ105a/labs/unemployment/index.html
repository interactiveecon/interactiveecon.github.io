ECON 105A — Unemployment

Unemployment — Matching Model

Explore how the labor market equilibrium responds to shocks to productivity, the labor force, the job-separation rate, and the wage.

Instructions

This lab lets you explore the matching model of unemployment from the lecture notes. The graph on the left shows labor demand and labor supply as functions of labor market tightness θ. The panel on the right shows worker flows between employment and unemployment in steady state.

Use the sliders to change four parameters:

  1. Productivity (A). A labor-demand shock — how productive workers are.
  2. Labor force (H). A labor-supply shock — the total number of workers.
  3. Job-separation rate (s). A labor-supply shock — the fraction of employed workers losing their jobs each month.
  4. Wage (W). A labor-demand shock — what firms pay workers.

Use the preset scenarios to see how recognizable real-world events show up in the model. Toggle the baseline comparison to show the original equilibrium alongside the new one. Toggle frictional vs. rationing to decompose total unemployment into its two types.
