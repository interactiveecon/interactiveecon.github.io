ECON 105A — Money

Money Cascade

Watch fractional-reserve banking create money round by round. Adjust the reserve ratio and currency-deposit ratio to see how the money multiplier emerges from a geometric series of loans and deposits.

Instructions

Use the sliders to explore how the money supply is created by fractional-reserve banking. Watch the cascade of deposits and loans round by round, and see how changing the reserve-deposit ratio (rr) or the currency-deposit ratio (cr) changes the total amount of money created.

Things to try:

  1. Start with the textbook case. Leave the defaults (B = $1000, rr = 0.20, cr = 0.00). Press Play and watch the cascade. Notice that the money supply converges to $5000 = $1000 × (1/0.20).
  2. Change the reserve ratio. Drag rr from 0.20 down to 0.10. Watch the multiplier double from 5 to 10. Why does a lower reserve ratio create more money?
  3. Add currency leakage. Drag cr from 0 up to 0.50. Watch the cascade — what happens to the deposits at each round? What happens to the money supply?
  4. Match the textbook example. Set B = 800, cr = 0.80, rr = 0.10. The money supply should converge to $1600, matching the example in §3.1 of the lecture notes.
  5. Notice the reserves. No matter how you set the sliders, the total reserves across all banks stays equal to rr × D. The cascade doesn't create reserves — it creates deposits.

The Cascade

Round 0 — Monetary base enters. The Fed adds $1,000 to the system. With cr = 0, the public deposits the full $1,000 at Firstbank.

What you're watching. When the Fed adds dollars to the monetary base, that money enters the banking system as a deposit. The bank keeps a fraction rr as reserves and lends out the rest. That loan becomes a new deposit at another bank, which keeps rr and lends out the rest. The geometric series of loans across all rounds is what creates the money supply.

Why the multiplier formula has that shape. With cr = 0, all the money created stays in the banking system as deposits, so the multiplier is the clean 1 / rr from the geometric series. When cr > 0, some money leaks out as currency at each round — that currency adds to the money supply (because M = C + D) but stops circulating through the banking system. The net effect is the full multiplier (cr + 1) / (cr + rr).
