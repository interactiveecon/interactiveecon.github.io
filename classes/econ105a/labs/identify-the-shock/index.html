Identify the Shock

Reverse-direction practice: read the loanable funds diagram before and after a shock, and identify which shock caused the change.

Instructions

You'll see a loanable funds market before and after a shock. Your job is to identify which shock caused the change. Read both the graph and the table of changes carefully — sometimes two different shocks move the interest rate in the same direction, but affect equilibrium investment differently.

  1. Read the setup. The savings model (S fixed or S(r)) is shown in the badge above the graph.
  2. Look at the diagram. The original equilibrium is drawn with solid curves; the new equilibrium is drawn with a dashed curve in a different color. Only one curve shifts.
  3. Check the changes table. It reports how r, equilibrium S = I, and disposable income Y − T changed. The Y − T row is what separates a tax shock from a government-purchases shock when both would shift the savings curve in the same direction — only a tax change moves Y − T.
  4. Choose one of four shocks. Click Submit Answer.
  5. Read the feedback. The correct channel is explained, along with what would have been different if any of the distractors had been the cause.

A full session is 8 problems. Your first-attempt score is recorded for the discussion summary.
