ECON 105B — Expectations

Expected vs. Unexpected Policy

See why an anticipated policy change moves prices but not output, and an unanticipated one does both.

Instructions

The economy starts in long-run equilibrium at point A (output Ȳ = 50, price level P₁ = 50, real interest rate r₁ = 50). A policy shock hits. Whether the public anticipated it decides whether short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) moves in the same period as aggregate demand (AD) — and that decides whether output moves at all. Work through the same shock under both cases and watch all three diagrams stay in agreement.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Does SRAS shift in the same period as AD? Yes or No — the mechanism that separates anticipated from unanticipated policy.
  2. Identify the short-run equilibrium. Pick A, B, or C — the same letter labels all three panels at once.
  3. Set expected prices to restore full employment. Enter the expected price level that returns output to Ȳ.
  4. Identify the long-run equilibrium. Pick A, B, or C.
  5. Compare the long-run real interest rate to the start. Higher, lower, or unchanged — the payoff that separates monetary neutrality from fiscal crowding out.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answers. If an answer is incorrect, an explanation is shown; you can revise and resubmit before your final submission.
