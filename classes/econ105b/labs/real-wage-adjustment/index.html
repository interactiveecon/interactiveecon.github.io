ECON 105B — Aggregate Supply

Real Wage Adjustment

Watch the mechanism that drives long-run output back to the natural rate Ȳ after a rise in the price level: the real wage W/P is eroded, workers demand higher nominal W, marginal cost shifts up, and the representative firm ends at the same output at a higher price.

Instructions

This lab makes concrete the lecture's claim that long-run output returns to Ȳ after a price shock. The mechanism rests on three facts:

  1. Workers care about the real wage W/P, not the nominal wage W.
  2. Firms hire labor until MPL = W/P. MPL falls as more labor is hired (diminishing returns); at the natural level of employment it equals 10 — the level set by capital and labor.
  3. When P rises with a sticky wage, the real wage falls, so firms hire more labor and MPL slides down to match — output rises above Ȳ. Workers then demand a higher nominal W; as it rises, the real wage, employment, MPL, and output all return to their natural levels, leaving only the price level higher.
