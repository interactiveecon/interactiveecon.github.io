ECON 105B — Expectations

Expectations & Disinflation

Work out what it costs to bring inflation down to target when expectations are only partly credible.

Instructions

In this lab you practice the arithmetic of disinflation. Inflation last period came in above the Fed's target. The Fed wants inflation at target this period, and the only instrument in the model is the unemployment rate. Because the public forms expectations as a weighted average of last period's inflation and the announced target, how much unemployment the Fed needs depends entirely on how much weight the public still puts on the past — that is, on how credible the Fed is.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Expected inflation. Compute Eπt from last period's inflation, the target, and the weight e on the past.
  2. Required unemployment rate. Solve the short-run Phillips curve for the unemployment rate ut that delivers inflation exactly at target.
  3. Output growth. Use Okun's law to translate that unemployment rate into a rate of output growth.
  4. Sacrifice ratio. Compare output forgone relative to trend with the reduction in inflation achieved.
  5. Next period. State the unemployment rate in period t+1 once inflation has reached the target and the Fed holds it there.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available.
