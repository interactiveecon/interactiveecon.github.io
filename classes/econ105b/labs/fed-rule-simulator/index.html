ECON 105B — Stabilization Policy

Fed Rule Simulator

Be the Fed. Choose the weights in the reaction function r = αY + βP + γZ, run your rule against a battery of demand and supply shocks, and see how it scores on output stability vs. price stability.

Instructions

The Fed's dual mandate is to stabilize output (around the natural rate Ȳ = 50) and prices (around the target P* = 50). The Fed's rule is r = αY + βP + γZ, where α, β, γ are weights that you, the policymaker, choose.

  1. Pick your weights. α puts pressure on output gaps, β on price gaps, γ is the response to an observable demand-side signal Z the Fed can lean against proactively.
  2. Pick a shock battery. Demand-only, supply-only, or mixed.
  3. Run. The economy faces each shock in sequence under your rule. The scorecard reports two numbers: output volatility and price volatility — never collapsed into one. The whole lesson is the tradeoff.
  4. Compare. The last three runs stay on screen so you can see how tweaking the weights moves the two numbers in opposite directions.

Try the same rule against a different battery. A rule tuned for demand shocks misbehaves under supply shocks — that's the stagflation discussion from lecture.
