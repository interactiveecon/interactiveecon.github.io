ECON 105B — The Open Economy

The Small Open Economy in the Long Run

Take a small open economy with perfect capital mobility apart in five steps: total saving, investment at the world interest rate, net capital outflow, and the real exchange rate that clears the market for foreign exchange — then say in words what the signs you computed actually mean.

Instructions

You are handed a small open economy: output is fixed at full employment, the domestic real interest rate is pinned to the world rate r* by perfect capital mobility, and net exports depend on the real exchange rate ε. Everything real in this economy follows from that.

Steps unlock one at a time. You have to commit an answer to a step before the next one opens and before any help for that step becomes available — that is the point of the lab. Once you have the exchange rate, the two-panel diagram from the lecture notes is drawn from your numbers.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Total saving. Evaluate consumption at the world interest rate, then find S = Y − C − G.
  2. Investment. Evaluate the investment function at the world interest rate.
  3. Net capital outflow. Find S − I — and pay attention to its sign.
  4. The real exchange rate. Clear the market for foreign exchange: set S − I equal to NX(ε) and solve for ε.
  5. Interpretation. Three questions about the economy you just solved: trade balance, lender or borrower, and spending versus output.

Enter your answer and click Check Answer (or press Enter). All dollar amounts are in billions of dollars, r* is in percent, and ε is a unitless index. Round to two decimals. After you submit, a three-step help ladder opens — Hint, then Formula, then Worked solution. Using help is recorded but never penalized, and a step you got wrong stays open so you can fix it.
