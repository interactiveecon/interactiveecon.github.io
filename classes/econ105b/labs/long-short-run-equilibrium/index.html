ECON 105B — Long and Short-Run Equilibrium

Long and Short-Run Equilibrium

Apply demand and supply shocks, watch the economy move from long-run equilibrium A to short-run equilibrium B and back to long-run equilibrium C — with AD-AS and IS-LM moving together so you can see the transmission mechanism behind every shift.

Instructions

The economy starts in long-run equilibrium at point A (output Ȳ = 50, price level P₁ = 50). Use the sliders to apply shocks, then optionally apply policy and long-run price adjustment (expected inflation) to see where the economy settles.

  1. Shocks (top group) shift either AD, SRAS, or LRAS. Multiple shocks can be combined.
  2. Policy shifts AD via the money supply (M), government spending (G), or taxes (T).
  3. Expected inflation shifts SRAS up as workers demand higher nominal wages to match expected price rises — this is the long-run adjustment.
  4. Select A / B / C to highlight an equilibrium: A is the initial long-run point, B is the short-run point after the shock only, C is the new long-run point after policy and price adjustment.

The right panel shows the IS-LM diagram that sits behind every AD shift. When the price level changes, real money balances change and LM shifts in lockstep — so the same equilibrium label (A, B, C) appears at consistent (Y, r) and (Y, P).
