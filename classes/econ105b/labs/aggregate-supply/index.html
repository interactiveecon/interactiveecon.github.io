ECON 105B — Aggregate Supply

Aggregate Supply

Apply a shock to aggregate demand, then watch how it splits between the price level and output. The split depends on the share of firms with sticky prices — see the macro AS/AD diagram and the representative sticky and flexible firms react together.

Instructions

This lab connects the macro AS/AD diagram to the firms behind it. The slope of the short-run aggregate supply curve (SRAS) is set by the share s of firms with sticky prices.

  1. Choose the share of sticky-price firms (s). At s = 1 every firm is sticky and SRAS is flat. At s = 0 every firm is flexible and SRAS is vertical (same as the long-run aggregate supply curve, LRAS).
  2. Pick a demand shock (positive or negative) and click Apply Demand Shock.
  3. The left graph shows aggregate demand shifting and the new short-run equilibrium where AD′ crosses SRAS. The right graph shows a sticky-price firm and a flexible-price firm responding to the same shock.
  4. Click Show Long-Run Adjustment to watch SRAS shift up (as nominal wages and rents rise, raising firms' marginal cost) until output returns to its natural level Y* — leaving only a higher price level.

Key idea: with sticky-share s, a demand shock Δ splits cleanly as ΔY = s·Δ and ΔP = (1−s)·Δ in the short run, and entirely on prices in the long run.
