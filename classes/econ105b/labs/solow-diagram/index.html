ECON 105B — Economic Growth

Solow Model: Diagram Explorer

Move the five parameters of the Solow model and watch the diagram, the path the economy actually takes, the growth of log output, and the golden-rule trade-off all respond together.

Instructions

This lab is an exploration, not a problem set — there is nothing to submit and no answer to get wrong. The point is to build an intuition for which parameter does what, and for the difference between where the economy ends up and how it gets there.

The four panels show:

  1. The Solow diagram. Output per effective worker f(k̂), saving s·f(k̂), and break-even investment (δ+n+g)k̂. They cross at the steady state k̂*. The shaded bands at k̂* split output into investment and consumption.
  2. Transition path. Capital per effective worker against time, starting from two points you choose — one below the steady state and one above it.
  3. Growth in log output. Log output per effective worker and log output per worker against time. The gap between their slopes is the whole point of Chapter 9.
  4. The golden-rule trade-off. Steady-state consumption plotted against every possible savings rate.

What to try first:

  • Press Increase the savings rate and watch Panel B: growth restarts, then stops. Capital accumulation buys short-run growth, not long-run growth.
  • Turn on Show golden rule and check that the tangent to f(k̂) at k̂gr is parallel to the break-even ray. That parallel is the condition MPK = δ+n+g.
  • Press Remove technology (g = 0) and watch the hats disappear from every label. Chapter 8 is Chapter 9 with g set to zero.
  • Drag the two small triangles on Panel A's horizontal axis (or use the two Starting capital sliders) to change where the economy starts.
  • Use Pin current equilibrium before moving a slider, so the old curves stay on screen underneath the new ones — that is how the shift diagrams in the notes are drawn.

Watch the three growth rates in the readout while the diagram sits perfectly still. A steady state does not mean nothing grows.

Steady state, golden rule, and growth

Panel A — The Solow diagram
Saving crosses break-even investment at the steady state.
  • f(k̂) = k̂α — output, solid
  • s·f(k̂) — saving = investment, dashed
  • (δ+n+g)k̂ — break-even, dotted
  • golden rule — tangent, when shown

Curves are told apart by line style and by the label drawn at the right end of each one, not by colour alone. Labels are always visible — there is nothing to hover over.

Panel B — Transition path
Capital per effective worker over 100 periods, from two starting points.
  • Start A (below k̂*), solid
  • Start B (above k̂*), dashed
  • steady state, dotted

Both starts converge to the same k̂*, from opposite directions — the steady state is a sink.

Panel C — Growth in log output
Log output over time, along the Start A path.
  • log ŷ — per effective worker, solid
  • log y — per worker, dashed

During the transition both slopes are steep. Afterwards log ŷ is flat while log y keeps rising at rate g.

Panel D — The golden-rule trade-off
Steady-state consumption at every possible savings rate.
  • ĉ*(s) — steady-state consumption, solid
  • peak at s = α, dotted
