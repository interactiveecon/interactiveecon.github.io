ECON 105B — Long- and Short-Run Equilibrium

Transition Path: Impact to Long Run

Trace a shock from the moment of impact, through the short-run equilibrium at B, to the long run at C — one stage of the mechanism at a time, on stacked IS–LM and AS–AD diagrams that share the output axis. Covers goods-market, money-market, and cost shocks, including the permanent cost shock that moves LRAS.

Instructions

Each scenario is one shock, decomposed into the numbered stages of the course worksheet — ten for a demand shock, eight for a temporary cost shock. For each stage you build the causal chain, state what happens to the curve, check it, and then animate the figure to watch the economy move.

  1. Read the headline. It describes a shock to the goods market (G, T, c0, I0), to the money market (M, (M/P)d), or to costs. For a cost shock the brief also tells you whether the change is temporary or permanent — that is what decides where the economy ends up, and the diagram alone will not tell you.
  2. Build the chain. Put a variable in the well and set its direction with the ↑ / ↓ toggle. Then press → to lock that step and reveal the next one. You will not know how many steps there are until the chain ends.
  3. State what the curve does. When the chain finishes, → reveals two dropdowns: which curve, and whether it shifts or the economy moves along it.
  4. Check stage. Grading is all-or-nothing: every variable, every direction, in order, plus the curve statement. If something is wrong you are told which step, not the answer. Show answer fills the stage in and forfeits the point. Why? explains the economics.
  5. Animate to move the figure for that stage, then Next stage →. Curves and points for stages you have not reached are not drawn.

Keyboard: every drag has a keyboard equivalent. Tab to a pill, press Enter or Space to select it, Tab to a variable well, press Enter or Space to place it, and Delete or Backspace to clear it. The direction toggles, the → button, and every other control are ordinary buttons and selects.

Scoring: one point per stage answered correctly on the first check, so a scenario is worth as many points as it has stages. Your running score appears next to the scenario counter and is recorded to your session after every stage, so you can stop whenever you like.
