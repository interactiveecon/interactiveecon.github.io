ECON 105B — Long and Short-Run Equilibrium

Spot the Shock

Read the diagrams diagnostically. Each problem shows a completed A → B → C path on the linked AD-AS and IS-LM diagrams. Identify which shock could have produced it — the reverse of the standard equilibrium lab.

Instructions

The economy starts in long-run equilibrium at point A (Y = 50, P = 50). A shock moves it to a new short-run equilibrium at B. As wages and prices adjust (via expected inflation πe), the economy settles at a new long-run point C.

For each problem you'll answer two or three sub-questions:

  1. Variable. Which variable was shocked? Each problem shows one demand option (C, I, G, or T), one short-run supply option (wages, rental rate of capital, or tariffs), one long-run supply option (technology, labor supply, or capital stock), and monetary policy.
  2. Direction. Positive (the relevant curve shifted in the expansionary direction — right for AD / IS / LM / LRAS, down for SRAS) or negative (the curve shifted in the contractionary direction — left for AD / IS / LM / LRAS, up for SRAS).
  3. Permanence. Only shown for supply shocks — temporary (SRAS only) or permanent (LRAS shifts too)?

Each sub-question is graded independently. After submitting, targeted feedback explains the diagnostic that gives the answer away — for example, whether the interest rate rises or falls alongside output is what distinguishes a real-demand shock from a monetary one.
