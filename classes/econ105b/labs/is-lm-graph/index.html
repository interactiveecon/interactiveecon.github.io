ECON 105B — Review of Aggregate Demand

IS-LM Graph

Apply a randomly drawn shock to the economy, then prescribe the fiscal or monetary policy that fully offsets it. Watch all four panels — Investment, the Keynesian Cross, the Money Market, and IS-LM — update in real time.

Instructions

Each problem has two phases.

  1. Phase 1 — Identify the shock. You're given a real-world scenario (a news headline). Decide which model variable shifted — government spending (G), taxes (T), autonomous investment (I₀), or autonomous consumption (c₀) — and in which direction. Click the matching button. The shock is then applied to the model regardless of whether you picked correctly, so you can see the curve shifts.
  2. Phase 2 — Apply the policy. An institution (Congress or the Fed) wants to stabilise either output or the interest rate by changing one policy lever. Click the direction that actually achieves that target. The magnitude is computed for you exactly — only the direction is your choice.

The four panels (Investment, Keynesian Cross, Money Market, IS-LM) all share the same underlying parameters. Curves are drawn solid in blue at baseline, dashed in blue after the shock, and solid in green (or red, if you picked the wrong policy direction) after the policy.
