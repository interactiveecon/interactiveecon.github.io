ECON 104B — Asymmetric Information

Education as a Signal

Two workers — one high-productivity, one low-productivity — decide whether to attend college. Compare each worker's net benefits, see whether the market's wages succeed in sorting the two types, and if not, find the range of college-graduate wages that would.

Instructions

An employer cannot tell a high-productivity worker apart from a low-productivity worker by sight, but is willing to pay PH to a high-productivity worker and PL to a low-productivity worker (PH > PL). College is cheap for the high-productivity worker (cost CH) and expensive for the low-productivity worker (cost CL). Walk through the ten steps to decide whether the proposed pay schedule actually sorts the two worker types.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Question 1 (Steps 1–7). Suppose the market pays PH to college graduates and PL to non-graduates. Compute each worker's net benefit from attending and from not attending college, decide who attends, and decide whether the wages sort the types.
  2. Question 2 (Steps 8–10). Find the lowest wage to college graduates that still gets the high-productivity worker to attend, the highest wage that still keeps the low-productivity worker away, and decide whether sorting is feasible given that no firm will pay more than PH.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available.
