ECON 104B — Public Goods

Public Goods: Vertical Summation & Free-Riding

See why the optimal quantity of a public good is found by vertically summing individual marginal benefit curves — and why an unregulated market underprovides because each consumer free-rides on the other's purchase.

Instructions

Two consumers value a good. Consumer 1's marginal benefit is MB1(Q) = a1 − b1Q; Consumer 2's is MB2(Q) = a2 − b2Q. The constant marginal cost of producing one more unit is MC = m.

If the good is a public good (nonrival — every consumer enjoys the same unit simultaneously), the social marginal benefit is the vertical sum ΣMBi, and the efficient quantity is where this sum equals MC.

If the same MB curves described a private good (rival — each unit goes to one consumer only), the market demand would be the horizontal sum, and the efficient quantity would equal Q1 + Q2.

Use the controls to compare the two summation methods and to see how an unregulated market underprovides a public good because each consumer can free-ride on the other's purchase.

  1. Pick a preset to draw a fresh random pair of consumers — either symmetric (identical MB curves) or asymmetric (one consumer values the good more, randomly either Consumer 1 or Consumer 2).
  2. Adjust the MC slider to change how expensive each additional unit is.
  3. Toggle the view between Public Good (vertical sum) and Private Good (horizontal sum) and watch the sum curve transform.
  4. Answer the four questions below the graphs once you've explored.
