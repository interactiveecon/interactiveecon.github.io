ECON 104B — Game Theory

Solving a Sequential Game

Two players move one after the other. Solve the game by backward induction: at each level, find the best response of the player who moves, eliminate the branches they will never take, and propagate the surviving payoffs up to the next level. The subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium is the path that survives.

Instructions

You are given an extensive-form game with two players who alternate moves. The tree has between 2 and 4 decision levels. The orange number at each terminal node is Player A's payoff; the blue number is Player B's.

Solve the game by backward induction, one round at a time:

  1. Identify the best payoff. At the deepest level, click the payoff at the child node that is best for the player who moves at the parent decision node.
  2. Eliminate the unchosen branches. On each branch you want to eliminate, click its L / R label badge (the little circle with the letter). The branch will grey out.
  3. Check the round. When every decision node in this round has both a highlighted payoff and a greyed-out branch, press Check Round. If you got it right, the eliminated branches disappear and the surviving payoffs propagate up to the next level.
  4. Repeat one level higher until you reach the root. Once the whole game is solved, the tree expands back to its full size with the subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium path highlighted in green.

You can review hints and the worked answer after a wrong check.
