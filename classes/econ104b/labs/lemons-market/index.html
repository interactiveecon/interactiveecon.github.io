ECON 104B — Asymmetric Information

Market for Lemons

Buyers can't tell a plum (high-quality used car) from a lemon (low-quality) before purchase. Compute the buyer's expected value of a randomly chosen car and decide which types of cars survive in the market.

Instructions

A used-car market contains both plums and lemons. Sellers know which one they own, but buyers cannot tell the two apart until after the sale. With probability PL a randomly drawn car is a lemon, and with probability 1 − PL it is a plum.

Since buyers cannot distinguish the two, they will at most pay their expected value for a randomly drawn used car. Plums will only be brought to market if that expected value is at least the plum-seller's reservation price.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Expected value of a used car. Compute the buyer's expected value EV using the lemon probability and the buyer's valuations of the two types.
  2. What quality of cars are sold? Compare EV to the plum-seller's reservation price VPS and choose the resulting market outcome.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available.
