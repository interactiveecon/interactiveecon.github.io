Comparing Oligopoly Models

Slide the demand, cost, and number-of-firms parameters and watch how equilibrium price, quantity, profit, and welfare change across the five market structures from Chapter 11: monopoly, Cournot, Stackelberg, Bertrand, and perfect competition.

Instructions

This is an exploration lab — there is no single right answer to type in. Move the sliders, watch the equilibrium points and welfare bars update in real time, and use the patterns you see to answer the three reflection questions at the bottom.

The market has linear inverse demand P = a − b·Q and identical marginal cost MC = c for every firm. Cournot uses n symmetric firms; Stackelberg uses one leader and one follower (only defined when n = 2).

You will be asked to answer the following:

  1. Cournot convergence. As n grows, which of the other four models does the Cournot outcome look more and more like?
  2. Largest deadweight loss. Holding a, b, and c fixed, which market structure destroys the most surplus?
  3. Bertrand profit. Under Bertrand competition with identical goods and identical marginal cost, do firms earn positive economic profit?

Show the closed-form solutions used here

All formulas assume linear inverse demand P = a − b·Q and identical constant marginal cost MC = c for every firm. The competitive quantity Q* = (a − c)/b is the reference for consumer surplus and deadweight loss.

Monopoly / perfect collusion

Q = (a − c)/(2b),  P = (a + c)/2,  π = (a − c)² / (4b)

Cournot with n symmetric firms

Per firm: qi = (a − c) / [(n + 1)·b];  Q = n·(a − c) / [(n + 1)·b];  P = (a + n·c)/(n + 1);  industry π = n·(a − c)² / [(n + 1)²·b].

Stackelberg (1 leader, 1 follower; n = 2 only)

Leader qL = (a − c)/(2b);  follower qF = (a − c)/(4b);  Q = 3(a − c)/(4b);  P = (a + 3c)/4;  leader profit (a − c)²/(8b),  follower profit (a − c)²/(16b).

Bertrand (identical goods, identical MC)

Undercutting drives price to marginal cost:  P = c,  Q = (a − c)/b,  π = 0.

Perfect competition

Long-run zero-profit benchmark:  P = c,  Q = (a − c)/b,  π = 0.

Welfare

Consumer surplus CS = ½ · (a − P) · Q;  deadweight loss DWL = ½ · (Qcomp − Q) · (P − c) where Qcomp = (a − c)/b.
