ECON 104B — Price Discrimination

Can This Firm Price Discriminate?

For a firm to use a pricing strategy that charges different prices to different customers, two requirements must hold at the same time: the firm must have market power, and the firm must be able to prevent resale and arbitrage. Practice applying these two requirements to a series of real-world scenarios.

Instructions

You will be shown ten short scenarios, one at a time. For each, decide whether the firm in the scenario can use price discrimination.

Choose one of three answers:

  1. Yes — can price discriminate. The firm has market power and can prevent customers from reselling the product to one another.
  2. No — lacks market power. The firm is a price-taker (or close to it), so it has no ability to choose a price above marginal cost — let alone different prices for different customers.
  3. No — can't prevent arbitrage/resale. The firm could in principle set different prices, but low-priced buyers can resell to high-priced buyers, undoing the strategy.

After you submit, the lab will tell you which requirement is (or isn't) satisfied and why. Your score is tracked across the ten scenarios.
