ECON 104B — Asymmetric Information

Moral Hazard & Optimal Precaution

Choose the precautionary actions that maximize your net benefit, then see how purchasing insurance that covers a share of your losses reduces the marginal incentive to take care.

Instructions

You face a problem with concave total benefit TB(A) and convex total cost TC(A) in the number of precautionary actions A. First find the action level that maximizes your net benefit on your own. Then suppose you can buy insurance that pays for a fraction IC of any losses you experience — so the benefit of precaution that accrues to you shrinks to (1 − IC)·TB(A). Re-solve and compare.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Find the optimal number of actions without insurance. Derive MB and MC, set MB = MC, and solve for A*.
  2. Find the optimal number of actions with insurance. Form the after-insurance total benefit, derive the new MB, and re-solve for AIN.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available.
