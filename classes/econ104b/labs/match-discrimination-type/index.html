ECON 104B — Price Discrimination

Match the Discrimination Type to the Scenario

First-, second-, and third-degree price discrimination all let a firm charge different prices, but they differ in how customers get sorted into prices. For each scenario, decide which type of discrimination is at work — and pay close attention to whether the firm is doing the sorting (third-degree) or the customer is sorting themselves (second-degree).

Instructions

You will be shown ten short scenarios, one at a time. For each one, decide which type of price discrimination is being used.

Choose one of three answers:

  1. First-degree (perfect). The firm charges each customer something close to their own willingness to pay, using information it has about that specific individual.
  2. Second-degree (indirect). The firm cannot directly observe which type of customer it is dealing with, so it offers a menu of prices or options and lets the customer self-select into the option meant for their type (quantity discounts, versioning, bundles, two-part tariffs).
  3. Third-degree (segmenting / direct). The firm directly identifies group membership using an observable characteristic verified before purchase (student ID, age, residency, military status, location) and charges that group its own single price.

After you submit, the lab tells you the correct answer and names the sorting mechanism — who is doing the sorting, and on what basis. Your score is tracked across the ten scenarios.
