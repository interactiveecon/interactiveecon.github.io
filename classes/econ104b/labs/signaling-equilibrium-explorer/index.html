ECON 104B — Asymmetric Information

Signaling — Separating Equilibrium Explorer

Move the sliders to discover when education works as a signal of worker quality — and when it stops working.

Instructions

Two types of workers — high-productivity and low-productivity — choose whether to attend college. Employers cannot observe a worker's type, but they can see whether the worker has a degree. If a degree reliably indicates the high type, the labor market reaches a separating equilibrium.

Use the sliders to vary:

  1. Cost of education per year for each type (CH, CL) and the years required for a degree.
  2. Lifetime value produced by each type (VH, VL).
  3. The wage offered to college graduates (Pc).

The payoff panels show each type's net benefit from going to college vs. not. The status bar classifies the equilibrium. The graph shows the cost lines and the band of Pc values that produce separation.

Then answer the five questions below. Each question may ask you to set the sliders to a specific configuration before answering.

What is a signal?

A signal is a costly action that one party takes to reveal private information to another. In Spence's model, workers know their own productivity but employers do not. Education is costly, and if it is more costly per year for the low type, only high types will choose to acquire it — making the degree a credible signal. Three things can happen as you vary the parameters: separation (only high types go to college), pooling (both go, or neither goes), and — if the single-crossing condition is violated — a perverse outcome.

Guided questions

Five questions step you through what you can discover by moving the sliders. Each question appears after you answer the previous one.

Q1

Start at the textbook defaults. (Click Reset to textbook values if you have moved sliders.)

Q2

Drag Pc DOWN until the status bar switches away from SEPARATING. Then nudge it back up by $5k until separation is restored. Use that value to answer.

Q3

Now drag Pc UP until separation breaks the other way. Then back it down $5k until separation returns. Use that value.

Q4

Click Reset to textbook values. Then drag CL DOWN to $25,000 (equal to CH). Try every Pc value you can. What happens?

Q5

Suppose a new technology cuts education costs in half for everyone: both CH and CL drop to half their textbook values. Compare the width of the separating Pc range before and after.
