Discount Factor Threshold Explorer

Two firms in the same market face a Prisoner's Dilemma every period: each can cooperate (charge the high collusive price) or defect (undercut for a one-period gain). When the game is infinitely repeated, how patient must firms be for collusion to survive as a subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium under a Grim Trigger strategy? Edit the profit matrix, slide the discount factor, and compare the two profit streams.

Instructions

Payoffs are annual profits in millions of dollars, so higher is better. The PD inequalities T > C > P > S have an economic reading: a one-period undercut beats sustained collusion (T > C); collusion beats a price war (C > P); and a price war beats being the only firm holding the high price while your rival undercuts (P > S).

Under a Grim Trigger strategy (cooperate until anyone defects, then defect forever), collusion is sustainable as a subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium iff the discount factor satisfies

δ ≥ δ* = (T − C) / (T − P).

Use New Problem to draw a fresh payoff matrix. You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Threshold value. Compute δ* for the matrix on screen.
  2. Comparative statics on T. When temptation rises, does δ* go up or down?
  3. Comparative statics on P. When punishment becomes more painful, does δ* go up or down?
  4. Intuition at δ = 0. What is the only sustainable equilibrium when the future has zero weight?

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer.

Assessment questions

Question 1 of 4

Threshold for the current matrix. Using the values currently shown above, compute the discount-factor threshold δ* such that collusion is just barely sustainable. Enter δ* rounded to two decimal places (any answer within 0.01 of the true value counts as correct).

Question 2 of 4

Comparative statics on T. Suppose the temptation payoff T increases (a one-period undercut becomes more profitable), while C, P, and S are unchanged. Does the threshold δ* go up, down, or stay the same?

Question 3 of 4

Comparative statics on P. Suppose the punishment payoff P decreases (a price war becomes even less profitable), while T, C, and S are unchanged. Does the threshold δ* go up, down, or stay the same?

Question 4 of 4

Intuition at δ = 0. Set the slider to δ = 0. Which strategy profile is the only credible (subgame-perfect) equilibrium, and why?
