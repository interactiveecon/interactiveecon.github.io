Expected Utility vs. Expected Value

Pick between two lotteries on gut instinct, then see how three different utility functions — risk neutral, mildly risk averse, and strongly risk averse — rank the same pair.

Instructions

You will see two 50/50 lotteries with dollar payoffs. The expected values are not shown — eyeball them or do the math in your head.

You will work through the following steps:

  1. Make a gut-check pick. Without doing careful math, which lottery would you take? Submit your choice.
  2. Read the reveal. The lab computes expected value and expected utility under three utility functions and shows you which lottery each "personality type" prefers.
  3. Answer three comprehension questions on what the disagreement between risk-neutral and risk-averse rankings tells us.

For each comprehension question, pick an option and click Check Answer. If you're wrong you can revise your choice and submit again — your first and final answers are recorded separately.

Pick a lottery

A fair coin is flipped. Each lottery pays the high amount on heads and the low amount on tails. Without doing careful math, which one do you prefer?

Three personality types compare the same lotteries

For each utility function, expected utility EU = ½·v(low) + ½·v(high). The "Prefers" column shows which lottery that person would choose.

For a 50/50 lottery the chord midpoint sits at (EV, EU). When the curve is concave the chord drops below it, so EU is below v(EV) — and the gap is larger for the riskier lottery.
