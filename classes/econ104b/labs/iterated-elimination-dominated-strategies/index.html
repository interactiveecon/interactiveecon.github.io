ECON 104B — Game Theory

Iterated Elimination of Dominated Strategies

Apply the dominant-strategy logic from the lecture to larger games. Remove strictly dominated strategies one at a time and watch new dominations appear as the opponent's options shrink.

Instructions

You will be shown a 3×3 or 4×4 payoff matrix. Player A is the row player and Player B is the column player; each cell shows A's payoff, B's payoff.

Definitions.

  • Strict dominance. Strategy X strictly dominates strategy Y for a player if X gives that player a strictly higher payoff than Y against every surviving strategy of the opponent. A single tie (equal payoffs in any column for A, or any row for B) breaks strict dominance.
  • Weak dominance. Strategy X weakly dominates strategy Y if X gives at least as high a payoff in every surviving opponent strategy, and a strictly higher payoff in at least one. Weak dominance allows ties — but if every payoff is equal, neither strategy dominates the other.

Your task:

  1. Find a dominated strategy. Pick the player whose strategy is dominated, pick the strategy to eliminate, pick the surviving strategy of the same player that dominates it, and pick the type of dominance you're claiming (strict or weak). Click Check elimination.
  2. Repeat on the reduced game. A strategy that wasn't dominated before may become dominated once the opponent has fewer options.
  3. Click Done once no strategy is strictly or weakly dominated in the remaining game. Then mark each player's best responses in the surviving sub-game and list any pure-strategy Nash equilibria.

Caution with weak dominance. Unlike strict iterated elimination, iterating weak dominance can discard some Nash equilibria and the surviving sub-game can depend on the order of elimination. Use weak dominance only when no strict dominance is available, and be aware that the PSNE you identify afterwards may be a subset of the original game's PSNE.
