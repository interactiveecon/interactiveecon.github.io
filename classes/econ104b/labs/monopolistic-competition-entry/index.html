ECON 104B — Monopolistic Competition

Monopolistic Competition: Long-Run Entry

Each firm in a monopolistically competitive market faces a downward-sloping residual demand curve for its differentiated product. Positive economic profit attracts entry; entry shifts every firm's residual demand inward and flatter; long-run equilibrium is reached when residual demand is tangent to the average total cost curve and per-firm economic profit is zero.

Instructions

The left panel shows the representative firm's diagram: a downward-sloping residual demand curve d, the corresponding marginal revenue curve mr, the firm's marginal cost MC, and its U-shaped average total cost ATC. The right panel tracks per-firm economic profit across rounds of entry.

Step forward through time using the controls beneath the canvases:

  1. Year +1 (add an entrant) — increments the number of firms n by 1. Every existing firm's residual demand shifts left and becomes flatter (more elastic), because consumers now have more differentiated substitutes.
  2. Run to long run — advances time round-by-round until economic profit reaches zero (the long-run tangency).
  3. Reset to short run — returns the market to the initial number of firms n₀.

Watch the profit rectangle shrink as entry proceeds. The market reaches long-run equilibrium when the residual demand curve just kisses the ATC curve at the profit-maximizing quantity — note that this tangency point lies to the left of the minimum of ATC (the source of the excess-capacity result).
