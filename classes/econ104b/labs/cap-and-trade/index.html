ECON 104B — Externalities

Cap-and-Trade: Tradable Permits Market

Three firms with different marginal abatement costs face the same total emissions cap. Compare a uniform quota (each firm cuts the same amount) to a tradable-permits market, and see why trading reaches the same environmental outcome at lower total cost by equalizing marginal abatement costs across firms.

Instructions

Three firms — A, B, and C — each start with baseline emissions of 50 units. Each has a linear marginal abatement cost (MAC) curve of the form MAC(a) = β·a, where a is the firm's abatement (units cut below baseline). Firms with a steeper MAC find abatement more expensive at the margin.

The regulator sets a total emissions cap. There are two ways to enforce it:

  1. Uniform quota. Each firm is required to abate an equal share of the total.
  2. Tradable permits. Each firm is issued an equal number of permits, but firms can buy and sell them. Trading clears at the price where the marginal abatement cost is the same across all firms.

Use the controls to switch between symmetric and asymmetric firms, set the cap, and flip between the two regimes. Click New Problem for a freshly randomized scenario (the high-cost firm is randomly assigned, so check the MAC slopes before answering). Watch how the per-firm MACs equalize at the permit price under trading, and how the comparison strip reports the resulting cost savings. Then answer the four questions below.
