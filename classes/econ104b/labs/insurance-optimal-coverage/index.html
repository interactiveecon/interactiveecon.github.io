ECON 104B — Uncertainty & Insurance

Optimal Insurance Coverage

A household with wealth m faces a possible loss L with probability pa. Choose how much insurance K to buy at premium rate γ, then decide whether the insurance offered is actuarially fair or unfair.

Instructions

The household has two states of the world — accident (probability pa) and no accident (probability 1 − pa). It chooses an insurance purchase K at premium rate γ to maximize expected utility. Work through the nine steps to set up the consumption budget, apply the optimality condition, solve for the optimal contingent-consumption bundle and the insurance purchase, and finally identify whether the insurance is fair.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Write down the budget constraints in each state. Consumption equals wealth net of the loss (if any), plus the insurance payout minus the premium.
  2. Eliminate K to express cna as a linear function of ca. This is the household's budget line in state-contingent consumption space.
  3. Apply the consumer's optimality condition to find the relationship between ca and cna, then solve for the optimal levels of ca, cna, and K.
  4. Decide whether the insurance is fair or unfair. Insurance is actuarially fair when the premium rate equals the probability of the accident (γ = pa); otherwise it is unfair.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available.
