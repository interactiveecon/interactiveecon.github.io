ECON 104B — Monopoly

Monopoly with a Per-Unit Tax

Solve a monopolist's profit-maximization problem, then trace how a per-unit excise tax shifts marginal cost and splits the burden between consumers and the firm.

Instructions

A single-price monopolist faces a linear inverse demand curve and a quadratic total cost function. The government then levies a per-unit excise tax. Work through the nine steps to find the pre-tax equilibrium, the post-tax equilibrium, and the share of the tax borne by consumers versus the firm.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Find the firm's profit-maximizing output and price (no tax). Derive MR and MC, set MR = MC to find Q*, then read P* off the demand curve.
  2. Find the new equilibrium after a per-unit tax. Form the after-tax cost function, derive the new marginal cost, and re-solve for Q′ and P′.
  3. Determine tax incidence. Compute the share of the tax borne by consumers and the share borne by the monopolist.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available.
