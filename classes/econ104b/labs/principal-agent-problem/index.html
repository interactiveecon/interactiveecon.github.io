ECON 104B — Asymmetric Information

The Principal-Agent Problem

A firm (the principal) hires a worker (the agent) and chooses how to pay them. Compute the worker's net benefit and the firm's profit under a fixed salary and under a revenue-share contract, then find the minimum revenue share that aligns the worker's interest with the firm's.

Instructions

If the employee puts in high effort, the employer earns revenue RH; with moderate effort, the firm earns RM, where RH > RM. Effort is costly to the worker: CH > CM. The worker chooses the effort level that maximises their net benefit (earnings minus cost), and a principal-agent problem occurs when the worker's choice does not maximise the firm's profit.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Fixed Salary. Under a flat wage F, compute the worker's net benefit and the firm's profit for each effort level, then decide whether a principal-agent problem exists.
  2. Revenue-Share Contract. When the worker keeps a share P of revenue, repeat the calculation and again decide whether a principal-agent problem exists.
  3. Minimum Revenue Share. Find the threshold revenue share P1 above which the worker is willing to expend high effort.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available.
