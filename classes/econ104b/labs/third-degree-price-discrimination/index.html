ECON 104B — Price Discrimination

Third-Degree Price Discrimination

A monopolist serves two distinct customer types with different inverse demand curves. Derive the marginal revenue in each market, set MR equal to marginal cost in each, and solve for the profit-maximizing quantity and price charged to each type.

Instructions

In this lab you'll practice the four-step procedure a price-discriminating monopolist follows when it can identify and separate two groups of customers. You'll be given each group's inverse demand curve and the firm's total cost function.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Find the marginal revenue curve for each customer type. Differentiate total revenue with respect to quantity in each market.
  2. Find the firm's marginal cost curve. Differentiate the total cost function with respect to total output.
  3. Find the profit-maximizing quantity in each market. Set marginal cost equal to marginal revenue in each market and solve for Q.
  4. Find the profit-maximizing price in each market. Plug the optimal quantity back into the corresponding inverse demand curve.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available.
