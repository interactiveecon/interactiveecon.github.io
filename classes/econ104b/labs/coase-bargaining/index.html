ECON 104B — Externalities

Coase Theorem: Bargaining Over Externalities

A factory and a neighboring resident bargain over pollution. Toggle who holds the property rights, change the curves, and see why — when transaction costs are low — private negotiation reaches the same efficient quantity Q* either way. What changes is the distribution of payments, not the level of pollution.

Instructions

A factory's marginal benefit from polluting is MB(Q) = a − bQ; the resident's marginal external cost from pollution is MEC(Q) = c + dQ. The efficient quantity Q* sets MB = MEC.

Use the controls to:

  1. Toggle who owns the right. If the factory has the right, the no-bargain quantity is Qmax (where MB = 0). If the resident has the right, it is 0.
  2. Choose a curve preset. Steep MEC, Steep MB, and Balanced highlight different shapes. Custom randomizes the curves so you can practice on a fresh problem.
  3. Adjust transaction costs. Per-unit cost τ shrinks the bargaining range. At high enough τ, bargaining breaks down and the no-bargain outcome persists.
  4. Adjust the surplus split. Where in the bargaining range the payment lands depends on bargaining power. This changes who gets what but not the quantity.

Watch the left canvas: Q* stays put when you toggle the rights; only the starting point and bargaining direction change. Watch the right canvas: the parties' net positions swap dramatically, but total surplus is invariant to the rights toggle (subject to transaction costs). Then answer the four questions below.
