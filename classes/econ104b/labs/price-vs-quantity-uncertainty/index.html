ECON 104B — Externalities

Price-Based vs. Quantity-Based Interventions Under Uncertainty

Compare a Pigouvian tax to a quantity cap when the regulator is uncertain about the marginal abatement cost (MAC) curve. See how the relative slopes of MAC and the marginal cost of pollution (MCP) — not the direction of the error — decide which instrument has lower deadweight loss.

Instructions

The market generates pollution. Each unit polluted yields a marginal benefit equal to MAC(Q) = a − b·Q (the marginal abatement cost — the polluter's willingness to pay to avoid being cut by one unit). Each unit of pollution inflicts marginal social damage MCP(Q) = c + d·Q. The true social optimum is where MAC = MCP.

The regulator must set policy before observing the true MAC. Their estimate of the MAC intercept may be too high or too low. They can either set a Pigouvian tax (a price) or a quantity cap. Once policy is set, polluters respond to the true MAC.

Use the controls to compare both instruments side by side:

  1. Pick a scenario preset to set the slopes of MAC and MCP. Or use the slope sliders directly.
  2. Move the estimation-error slider to give the regulator over- or under-estimates of the true MAC.
  3. Watch the two DWL triangles. Whichever is smaller is the better instrument under those slopes.
  4. Answer the three questions below the graphs once you've explored.
