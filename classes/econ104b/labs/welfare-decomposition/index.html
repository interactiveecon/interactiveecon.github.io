ECON 104B — Monopoly

Welfare Decomposition under Market Power

When a market shifts from perfect competition toward monopoly, consumer surplus is split into three pieces: the smaller triangle consumers still get, a rectangle transferred to the firm, and a triangle of pure deadweight loss that no one captures.

Instructions

The diagram shows a linear inverse demand curve, a faded marginal-revenue curve, and a horizontal marginal-cost line. The three shaded regions are the building blocks of welfare analysis under market power:

  1. A — Consumer surplus. The triangle above the current price and below demand.
  2. B — Producer surplus (transfer). The rectangle between MC and the current price. This area moves from consumers to the firm as price rises above MC; it is not destroyed.
  3. C — Deadweight loss. The triangle between the current quantity and the competitive quantity, bounded by demand above and MC below. Pure waste — neither side gets it.

Drag the Market Power slider to move continuously from competition (P = MC) to full monopoly (where MR = MC). Watch how A shrinks, B grows out of A, and C appears.
