ECON 104B — Asymmetric Information

Adverse Selection vs. Moral Hazard

Classify each scenario by what kind of information is hidden and when the friction occurs. The timing rule — before the contract vs. after the contract — is the cleanest way to tell the two problems apart.

Instructions

You will be shown eight short scenarios, one at a time. For each one, work through three steps:

  1. What is hidden? Decide whether the hidden item is a type (a fixed characteristic of the person, like risk or quality) or an action (something they choose to do, like effort or precaution), or whether the information is symmetric and nothing is hidden.
  2. When does the friction occur? Decide whether the friction shows up before the contract is signed or after — or whether there is no contract in this scenario.
  3. Your classification. Pick one of Adverse Selection, Moral Hazard, Both, or Neither.

The first two selections are scaffolding to help you reason — only the final classification is graded. After you submit, the lab shows the correct answer and walks you through the diagnostic chain.

The timing rule

Adverse selection — hidden type (who they are), friction exists before the contract. The bad type self-selects in.
Moral hazard — hidden action (what they do), friction exists after the contract. Behavior changes once protected.
