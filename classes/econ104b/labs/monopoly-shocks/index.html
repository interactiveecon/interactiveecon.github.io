ECON 104B — Monopoly

Shock the Monopolist

A monopolist faces a linear demand curve and a constant marginal cost. Apply a shock — to costs, to the level of demand, or to its elasticity — and watch how the profit-maximizing price and quantity move.

Instructions

The diagram below shows a single-price monopolist in equilibrium: demand (D), marginal revenue (MR), and marginal cost (MC) all intersect to determine the profit-maximizing quantity Q* and price P*.

Click one of the shock buttons to perturb the environment. The diagram will animate from the old equilibrium to the new one. The original curves stay on screen (faded, solid) and the new ones are drawn on top (dashed). The narration panel summarises the change in words.

  1. MC increases / decreases. The horizontal MC line shifts up or down by $4. Trace what happens to Q* and P*.
  2. Demand increases (parallel shift out). D and MR both shift right by the same amount. Watch how Q* and P* move together.
  3. Demand becomes more / less elastic. D pivots through the original (Q*, P*) point, so it has the same value at the old equilibrium but a different slope. The monopolist's response is the interesting case — pay close attention to the price.

Then answer the six comprehension questions. Each has one correct answer; click Submit Answers when finished.
