ECON 104B — Monopoly

Why Marginal Revenue is Less Than Price

For a firm with market power, selling one more unit forces the price down on every unit sold. Marginal revenue is the net of two opposing effects — drag the point on the demand curve to see them, and watch the MR curve emerge.

Instructions

A monopolist faces the inverse demand curve shown below. To sell one more unit, the firm must lower price — and the lower price applies to every unit it sells, not just the extra one. Marginal revenue is the sum of two effects:

  1. Output effect (+). Revenue from the new unit at the new price. This is the green rectangle on the left graph.
  2. Price effect (−). Revenue lost on the inframarginal units because their price had to drop too. This is the red rectangle.

Drag the point on the demand curve (or use the slider / arrow keys) to change the quantity. The right graph plots MR = output effect − price effect at every quantity you visit, so the MR curve traces itself out as you sweep.
