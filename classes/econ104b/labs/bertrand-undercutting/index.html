ECON 104B — Oligopoly

Bertrand Price-Undercutting Race

Two firms sell identical products. Play a price-cutting game against a rival and watch prices spiral toward marginal cost — then add a capacity constraint and see why the spiral halts above cost.

Instructions

You play as Firm A. Your rival, Firm B, plays automatically: after every move you make, Firm B reacts by undercutting your price whenever undercutting is profitable for it. The two firms sell identical products, so customers always buy from whichever store has the lower price.

The lab has two modes:

  1. Mode 1 — Race to the Bottom. Neither firm has a capacity limit, so whichever firm posts the lower price serves the entire market. Click Undercut, Match, or Stay and watch the prices fall.
  2. Mode 2 — Capacity Limits Save the Day. Each firm can sell at most K units. Undercutting now only steals K customers; the rival keeps the rest at its higher price. See how this weakens the incentive to cut prices.

After playing both modes, answer the three reflection questions and click Submit Reflection to record your work.

Reflection Questions

Answer all three to record your work.

1. In the no-capacity Bertrand model with identical goods and identical marginal cost, what is the equilibrium market price?

2. Why does Firm B keep undercutting Firm A's price in the no-capacity model?

3. True or False: Adding a capacity constraint to the Bertrand model can sustain prices above marginal cost in equilibrium.
