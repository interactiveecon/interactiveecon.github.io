Why a Monopolist Never Prices in the Inelastic Region

Along a linear demand curve, elasticity changes continuously — elastic at the top, unit elastic at the midpoint, inelastic at the bottom. Drag the marker to see what happens to total revenue, total cost, and profit as you sweep through every part of the curve.

Instructions

The left graph shows a linear inverse demand curve, the marginal-revenue curve (twice as steep as demand), and a horizontal marginal-cost line. The elastic half of demand is drawn solid in blue; the inelastic half is drawn dashed in red.

The top-right graph plots total revenue TR(Q) = P(Q)·Q. The bottom-wide graph plots profit π(Q) = TR(Q) − MC·Q.

Drag the marker on the demand curve (or use the slider / arrow keys / Home / End / jump buttons). Watch what happens to TR and π as you move from the elastic region into the inelastic region.

  1. TR peak. Find the quantity where TR is highest — what is MR there?
  2. Inelastic region. What happens to TR if you raise P past the TR peak?
  3. Profit peak. Notice the profit-maximizing Q is to the left of the TR peak. Why?
