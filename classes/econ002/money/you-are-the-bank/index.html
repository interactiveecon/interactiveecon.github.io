ECON 002 — Money

You Are the Bank: T-Accounts and Money Creation

Fill in banks' T-accounts step by step and watch the money supply grow while total reserves stay fixed.

Instructions

You run the banks. The Fed creates $100 of new reserves, and you fill in each bank's T-account as the money moves from bank to bank. A dashboard tracks the whole banking system. Watch two numbers: the money supply (M) grows every time a bank makes a loan, but total reserves (R) never change after the Fed acts.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Part 1: The Fed injects reserves. Read the Fed's T-account and predict what happens to total reserves.
  2. Part 2: Walk the chain. Fill in three T-accounts for each of Banks 1, 2 and 3, run the rest of the chain, and sum up what happened.
  3. Part 3: The two levers. Use the two ways to change the money supply to solve two challenges.
  4. Part 4: Practice. Fill in T-accounts with new numbers. The New problem button makes fresh numbers, so you can practice as many times as you like.
  5. Check your understanding. Three final questions.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check or Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available. Show solution becomes available after two incorrect checks.

Part 1The Fed injects reserves

A bank's balance sheet

A bank's T-account lists what it owns on the left and what it owes on the right. Assets always equal liabilities.

  • Assets: Reserves (the bank's account at the Fed, plus vault cash) and Loans.
  • Liabilities: Deposits.

Assumptions:

  • The required reserve ratio is 20% (rr = 0.20).
  • Banks hold only the required reserves.
  • No one holds cash.
Step 1

An open market purchase

The Fed buys a $100 Treasury bond from a household. It pays by creating $100 of brand-new reserves. The household deposits the payment in Bank 1.

The Fed's T-account
AssetsLiabilities
Treasury bonds +$100 Bank reserves +$100

This is the only step in the whole lab where total reserves change, and it's the Fed that changes them.

After this step, Bank 1 has Reserves $100 and Deposits $100. The household's $100 deposit is new money, so the dashboard starts at M = $100 and R = $100.

The key result

Key result M equals m-hat times R, which is 1 over 0.20 times $100, which is $500. M = m × R = (10.20) × $100 = $500
“Financial intermediation does not change the total number of reserves in the economy. The Fed has complete control over the total number of reserves.”Unit 9 notes
