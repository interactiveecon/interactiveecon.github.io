ECON 002 — Unemployment

Does It Go Away on Its Own?

Follow three labor-market stories from short run to long run and discover why cyclical unemployment fades but structural unemployment persists.

Instructions

Right after they appear, cyclical and structural unemployment can look alike on a graph: the wage is above equilibrium and there is a gap between LS and LD. The difference is what keeps the wage up, and for how long. You will follow three stories over eight quarters.

  1. Pick a scenario. Recession, Minimum wage or Efficiency wages. In the recession you can also choose how the long run plays out.
  2. Advance to quarter 1. The shock hits. Before going further, predict whether this unemployment will disappear on its own, and click Lock in prediction.
  3. Run to the long run. Watch the unemployment rate, the natural rate and cyclical unemployment in the readout. At quarter 8 your prediction is checked and a row of the summary table fills in.
  4. Complete all three rows of the summary table.

Finish with the three Check your understanding questions. For each one, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, a hint and then the worked solution become available.
