    Recap: the definitions from the notes

    Frictional unemployment Unemployment that results from people switching jobs. The “right” job exists and there is a vacancy you can fill, but finding it takes time. Durations are typically short.
    Structural unemployment Unemployment caused by a change in the structure of the economy (such as technological change) that leaves too few jobs for a particular set of skills, or by wages kept above equilibrium (unions, the minimum wage, efficiency wages). Durations are typically long (27+ weeks); retraining is usually the only remedy.
    Cyclical unemployment Deviations from the natural rate of unemployment that occur during the business cycle. Sticky wages cause it in the short run; in the long run demand recovers or wages adjust.
    The natural rate of unemployment Frictional plus structural unemployment. The unemployment rate fluctuates around it. People who are not actively looking for work (retirees, discouraged workers) are out of the labor force and are not counted as unemployed.
