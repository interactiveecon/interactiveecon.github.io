ECON 002 — Aggregate Supply

Why Is the LRAS Vertical?

Let nominal wages catch up with prices and watch output return to potential GDP, then see why only a change in productivity can move the LRAS curve.

Instructions

In the short run, nominal wages are sticky. In the long run, workers win raises that make up for higher prices. This lab shows where that leaves the real wage (W/P), employment (L) and output (Y), and what can move potential output (YY bar). The three graphs at the top update as you work.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Part 1: Short run, then long run. Raise the price level, predict where output ends up, then let wages catch up one round at a time.
  2. Part 2: Trace the LRAS. Try five price levels and let wages adjust at each one, then draw the LRAS.
  3. Part 3: What can move the LRAS? Improve and then worsen technology, and follow each shock from the short run to the long run.
  4. Check your understanding. Three final questions.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available. For prediction questions, choose an answer and click Lock in before the answer is shown. Each step unlocks when the one before it is done.

Click New problem at any time for a new production function, a new starting point and a new price rise. All three parts start over with the new numbers.
