ECON 002 — Aggregate Supply

From MPL to the SRAS Curve

Use the marginal product of labor and the real wage to find how many workers firms hire, trace out the upward-sloping SRAS curve, and see how a change in nominal wages shifts it.

Instructions

Firms hire workers until the last worker's extra output (MPL) equals what that worker costs in goods (the real wage, W/P). The three graphs at the top show the labor market, the production function and the aggregate supply curve. They update as you work.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Where are we? Find the real wage, employment and output at W = $20 and P = 2.
  2. Prices rise, wages don't. Predict how firms respond, then work out the new numbers.
  3. Trace the SRAS. Move the price level, draw the curve, and put the causal chain in order.
  4. Workers win a raise. Predict what a higher nominal wage does to the SRAS curve.
  5. Sandbox. Change W and P yourself.
  6. Check your understanding. Three final questions.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check. If your answer is incorrect, hints and the worked solution are available. For prediction questions, choose an answer and click Lock in before the answer is shown. Each step unlocks when the one before it is done.

Click New problem at any time for a new production function and new wages and prices. The steps start over with the new numbers.
