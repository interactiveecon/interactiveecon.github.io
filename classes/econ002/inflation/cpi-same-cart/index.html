ECON 002 — Inflation

Same Cart, New Year

Price the same cart of goods in three different years, then build the CPI and the inflation rate step by step.

Instructions

The CPI answers one question: how much more does the same cart cost than it did in the base year? You will do the three steps from the lecture example yourself, with 2023 as the base year.

You will be asked to answer the following questions:

  1. Step 1: Cost of the basket. Fill in the 2023 receipt line by line, then total the 2024 and 2025 receipts.
  2. Step 2: The CPI. Turn each year's cost of the basket into an index with 2023 = 100.
  3. Step 3: The inflation rate. Find the percent change in the CPI from one year to the next.

For each step, enter your answer and click Check Answer. You can type answers with or without $, commas or %. If your answer is incorrect you get a hint, and then Show solution becomes available.

New cart draws new prices. Lecture example loads the prices from the notes so you can check your notes packet. Reset clears your answers but keeps the prices. Finish with the three Check your understanding questions.

Prices: new random cart.

Meet the cart

The Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks the price of a basket meant to represent what a typical urban consumer buys. Our basket has four goods. The quantities come from the base year (2023) and never change. That's the whole trick: if the stuff stays the same, any change in the total cost must come from prices.

    The CPI

    CPI = 100 × Cost of the basket in current period Cost of the basket in the base period CPI equals 100 times the cost of the basket in the current period divided by the cost of the basket in the base period.
    Step 3

    The inflation rate

    Finish Step 2 to unlock this step.

    π = New − Old Old × 100%, using the CPI this year (New) and last year (Old) Pi equals New minus Old, divided by Old, times 100 percent, using the CPI this year as New and the CPI last year as Old.
