ECON 002 — Inflation

Why Gas Matters More Than Movies

Discover why some price changes move the CPI more than others, and why economists watch core inflation.

Instructions

This lab uses the basket and 2023 prices from the lecture example, so every number matches your notes. You will see that a good's effect on the CPI depends on its share of spending, not just the size of its price change, and why economists also track core inflation.

You will work through:

  1. Where the money goes. See each good's share of base-year spending.
  2. Price-change duels. Predict which of two price changes raises the CPI more, lock in, then see the numbers.
  3. Sandbox. Move the price of each good and try two challenges.
  4. Headline vs. core inflation. Predict what happens when food and energy are removed, then compare the two.

Finish with the three Check your understanding questions. For each one, enter your answer and click Check Answer. If your answer is incorrect, a hint and then the worked solution become available.

Headline vs. core inflation

Food and energy prices bounce around a lot. Because they're a sizable part of the basket, they make measured inflation jumpy. Economists also track core inflation, which excludes food and energy, to see the underlying trend.

Twelve months of prices for the lecture basket. Month 0 is the 2023 prices. Headline uses the whole basket; core uses only rent and entertainment.
