ECON 002 — Inflation

Money, Goods, and Prices

See why prices rise when the money supply grows faster than the amount of goods, then use the quantity equation π = %ΔM − %ΔY.

Part 1The island economy: P = M ÷ Y

On this island, each dollar is spent exactly once. So the value of everything bought equals the money supply, and the average price of a good is the money supply divided by the number of goods.

The market M = $300 · Y = 100 goods · P = ? · bills per crate: not dealt yet

Each bill is $10. Each crate holds 10 goods. The bills are dealt out evenly, so the number of bills on a crate is the price of one good in dollars.

Predict: What happens to the average price of a good?

Chain: the money supply rises, so people want to buy more. There is the same number of goods, so the price level rises.
M↑ → people want to buy more → same number of goods → P↑
“Increasing the amount of money people have increases the amount they want to buy, and if the number of goods doesn't increase, prices will.”Unit 5 notes
Key result P equals M divided by Y, which is $600 divided by 100, which is $6. P = $600100 = $6

Part 2The growth-rate version: π = %ΔM − %ΔY

From levels to growth rates

Real economies don't double their money overnight. Each year the money supply grows by some percent, and output grows by some percent. The notes' quantity equation connects the growth rates: π = %ΔM − %ΔY.

π = %ΔM − %ΔY

In words: inflation (π) equals the growth rate of the money supply (M) minus the growth rate of output (Y).

The three cases
CaseRuleWhat it means
Inflation%ΔM > %ΔY → π > 0 Money is growing faster than goods.
Stable prices%ΔM = %ΔY → π = 0 Money and goods grow at the same rate.
Deflation%ΔM < %ΔY → π < 0 Goods are growing faster than money.
