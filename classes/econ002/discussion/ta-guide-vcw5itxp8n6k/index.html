Discussion Section TA Guide

Every discussion deck, in course order, with the throughline it is built around and how to run it. The decks themselves are student-facing; this page is not.

Please do not share this link

This page is unlisted: nothing on the site links to it, and it asks search engines not to index it. That keeps it away from students who are browsing normally, and it is the whole of the protection — there is no password.

Two things follow from that, and both matter:

  • Do not paste this URL into Canvas, a slide, a shared doc, or an email to students. Anyone who has the link can open it.
  • The site is built from a public GitHub repository, so a student who goes looking through the repository can find this file there regardless of the URL. Treat the page as not advertised rather than secret, and keep anything genuinely confidential off it.

The decks themselves contain no answer keys beyond what the reveal buttons show in class, so a student finding this page is an annoyance, not a disaster.

How to run a deck

All twelve work identically. Learn the controls once.

The one rule

Students write first; you reveal second. Every answer in every deck sits behind a button. Nothing uncovers itself and nothing auto-advances, so the pace is entirely yours.

When you reach a question slide, read it, then wait. Give the room genuine silence to write — longer than feels comfortable. The writing is the exercise; the reveal is just the check.

If you move away from a slide and come back, its reveals close again and the buttons come back. You can re-run any slide from scratch, and a deck opened twice never starts with an answer showing.

Controls

  • → Page Down Space — next slide
  • ← Page Up — previous slide
  • F — toggle fullscreen
  • On-screen prev / next and a slide counter sit in the bar at the bottom; a progress line runs across the top.
  • Watch out: if a button has keyboard focus, Space presses that button instead of advancing. Click an empty part of the slide first if you want Space to move you on.

Buttons you will meet

  • Show the answer / Show the reasoning — uncovers a gold reveal box.
  • Show the shift — draws the new curve, its label, the arrow naming the shock, the guides and the new equilibrium onto a diagram, all at once.
  • Play and Reset — run an animated figure and rewind it. Multi-stage adjustments pause between stages on purpose; let the pause land rather than talking over it.
  • A spent button greys out but stays put, so a second click never lands on the next answer by accident.

Practical notes

  • Each deck is a single file with no network calls. It runs from this site, and it also runs offline if you save it — worth doing if the room’s wifi is unreliable.
  • If a machine is set to reduce motion, animations skip the sweep and jump straight to the finished state. Nothing is lost.
  • Long slides scroll rather than clip. If something looks cut off, scroll the slide.
  • Questions are numbered continuously (Q1, Q2, …) so you can say “we are on Q7” and everyone knows where they are.

Slide types

The same eight types recur in every deck. Each is signalled by a colored label and a heading, not by color alone.

  • Divider Dark navy, opens each part. Carries a “Part N of M” eyebrow, the progress trail, and the deck’s throughline. Good moment to say where you are going.
  • Prompt Blue. A numbered question asking students to write something — a mechanism, a comparison, a chain of reasoning.
  • Predict Red. Asks for a committed guess before any computing. The point is to be wrong on paper and notice it. Do not let anyone skip to the diagram.
  • Reveal Gold, behind a button. States the answer and, more importantly, the reasoning. Read the mechanism aloud, not just the number.
  • Diagnose A silent poll: three numbered sentences, one subtly flawed. Students pick a number on paper or by hand, then you reveal. The flawed one usually reaches a true-sounding conclusion by bad reasoning, so ask for the flaw and not the verdict.
  • Quick check Multiple choice with a revealable correct option. Faster than a diagnose slide; use it to keep momentum.
  • Animated figure A diagram that moves when you press Play, with a live readout of the numbers. Narrate what is moving and why while it runs.
  • Close A fill-in-the-blank sentence students finish in their own words. Collect a couple aloud if there is time; it is the best signal of whether the hour landed.

Pacing

Each deck is built for one 50–75 minute section.

  • Budget backwards from the last part. The final part of every deck is where the unit’s payoff sits. If you are running long, compress the recap material early rather than rushing the end.
  • Do not skip the predict slides to save time. They are short, and they are what makes the reveal land. Cutting the writing and keeping the answer inverts the whole design.
  • Diagnose slides are the first thing to drop if you are genuinely out of time — they are self-contained. Say which number was wrong, say why in one line, and move on.
  • Getting through fewer slides properly beats finishing. Nobody is examined on slide count.
